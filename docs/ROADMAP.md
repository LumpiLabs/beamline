# Beamline roadmap

Each milestone ends with something that runs on the real devices: the Fire TV Stick
4K Max (Fire OS 7), a Pixel 8, a Galaxy S26+ and an iPad.

| Milestone | Scope | Done when | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M0 Foundation** | Android SDK on the dev PC, Gradle project with `protocol`, `sender` and `receiver` modules, CI build, adb installs through Gradle. | Both apps build in CI and install on the Fire TV and a phone. | Done locally; CI runs once the repository is pushed. |
| **M1 Video** | Discovery, TCP session, H.264 capture and playback, start and stop, rotation, Quick Settings tile, fill or fit the screen, matching the TV's refresh rate to the video (50 Hz for 25 fps). | Pixel 8 and Galaxy S26+ mirror smoothly to the TV at up to 1080p. | Done on the Galaxy S26+ and a Pixel 8 Pro (Android 16): a 25 fps video with sound in landscape, the TV at 50 Hz, no late or dropped frames over a minute, 533 ms behind the phone with sound in sync. |
| **M2 Audio** | Opus capture and playback, A/V sync, automatic reconnect. | Video with in-sync sound plays for 30 minutes without drift or drop-outs. | Done on the Galaxy S26+: 30 minutes of video, sound in sync (median 0 ms), no reconnects and no dropped frames. |
| **M3 Robustness** | TLS with numeric-comparison pairing, adaptive bitrate, HEVC, statistics overlay, clear error messages, an optional low-latency mode for presenting and games. | Recovers from Wi-Fi hiccups without restarting; low-latency mode stays under 150 ms on 5 GHz Wi-Fi. | Work on the Fire TV and the Galaxy S26+: TLS with pairing, reconnecting after a Wi-Fi drop, the statistics overlay, HEVC (a switch in the phone app), adaptive bitrate, and the low-latency mode (a switch in the phone app; 63 to 65 ms behind the PC test sender). Open: measuring the low-latency mode with a phone. |
| **M4 AirPlay** | UxPlay built with the NDK for armeabi-v7a and arm64-v8a, JNI bindings, shared player screen. AirPlay video too: the AirPlay button in a video app sends the video itself instead of the screen, so it keeps playing while the iPad is locked. | The iPad mirrors with sound through the same TV app, and a video sent with the AirPlay button keeps playing with the iPad locked. | Step 1 of 4 works with a Mac (macOS 27) and an iPad Pro: they list the TV, mirror, and decrypted video and sound reach the TV app (logged, not shown yet). Next: picture and sound on screen, the PIN, AirPlay video. |
| **M5 Polish** | TV: idle screen, D-pad settings, start on boot, busy handling, German and English text. Phone: an option to keep the phone awake (screen dimmed) while mirroring, because Android stops sending the picture when the phone locks. | Usable end to end with only the TV remote and the phones. | Works on the devices: German text in both apps, the phone keep-awake, the D-pad settings (fill, statistics, letting another phone take over, forgetting paired phones), on the Fire TV a paired phone opening the closed TV app, also after the TV restarted, and busy handling: a second phone hears "busy", or takes over where the TV allows it (tried on the Fire TV with two PC test senders). Open: a phone being taken over, seen on a real phone. |
| **M6 Release** | Signed release APKs on GitHub, user guide, F-Droid metadata, reproducible builds. | Someone else can install it from the README alone. | Store texts in English and German (`*/fastlane`); screenshots and the rest planned. |
| **M7 Laptops** | Macs already work through AirPlay (M4). A small Windows sender: screen or window capture with sound, hardware H.264, the same protocol and pairing as the phone app. | A Windows laptop mirrors a browser tab with sound to the TV. | Works on a Windows laptop (`windows-sender`, NVIDIA encoder) with the Fire TV: a whole screen of browser windows with sound, 60 fps with no late or dropped frames over a minute, 505 ms behind the laptop with sound in sync, or 75 to 85 ms in low-latency mode; a sleeping TV wakes and opens the app. Open: a video in a browser tab with its sound (seen so far: browser windows, and a tone played by Windows), window capture, the mouse cursor, AMD and Intel encoders. |
